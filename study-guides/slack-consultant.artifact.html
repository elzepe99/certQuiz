01The exam, factually

A consulting exam, and a deck that can only half-teach it

This certification tests how you run a Slack engagement — discovery, design, migration, governance, training — more than which toggle does what. That makes a practice deck unusually weak evidence of readiness: most of what it asks is advice, and the deck's wrong options are often absurd enough to eliminate without knowing anything. §3 says how much of your score the deck can genuinely predict.

How the outline was found — the part that generalises

The obvious place to look is Slack's own site; the outline is not there. Slack's consultant credential is a Salesforce certification now, and its exam guide is a Salesforce Help article in the same 0052989xx block as the others, beside Slack Developer (005298987) and Slack Administrator (005298990). One web search restricted to help.salesforce.com found it, and its top hit was a named Trailhead redirect — a URL of the form trailhead.salesforce.com/help?article= followed by the guide's title with hyphens — that lands on the numeric id. That form is worth trying first for any Salesforce exam. The guide renders fully with get_page_text; there is no PDF.

The audience list tells you what kind of exam this is

Implementation consultant, engagement manager, enablement consultant, change management consultant, learning consultant. The guide asks for project management, change management, discovery, launch support and post-launch enablement. It recommends a single Trailmix for study and says to search Help for the outline's topics. There is no list of documents that would settle this exam, which is the honest shape of §3.

03How much of this deck any page can check

Every one of the 37 questions has a rendered Slack URL in its references, so the deck reads 37/37 cited, the same number as a fully checked Salesforce deck. Here it means less. The 2026-08-18 fact-check sorted the deck into three kinds of question, and only the first kind is what "cited" means elsewhere.

KindItemsWhat the citation does
Decisive15A Slack page states the fact the key turns on — roles, channel-management permissions, app approval, DLP, SCIM, troubleshooting steps. Or, for three items, states that the fact the deck assumed is not true
Supported10A page points the same way as the key — naming conventions, project channels, analytics columns, org-level policy — but choosing between the options is still best practice
Judgment12Orienting only. Consulting and training advice; the reference is Slack's "launching Slack" admin resource or a migration blog post, and no page could settle it

The twelve judgment items

Discovery: 70cfc31f, 4c998d54, a401a105. Migration: 663ae7a0. Learning and Enablement: 7cdb2278, 20885bb7, 9f6ca95b, 0609296f, 24d2ac86, eaa94ffb, bd98bac0, 0c94d85c. Each says in its own explanation that its reference is orienting rather than decisive.

Why this matters for your score, not just the deck's

The judgment items are answerable because their wrong options are strawmen — "read the user manual during the session", "replace their computer's sound card". Getting them right proves you can spot a bad option, not that you know the good one. The real exam's judgment items will have four plausible options. A high score on this deck's Discovery and Learning items should move your confidence very little.

How to answer a judgment item when the options are all plausible

This is the skill the deck cannot drill. The pattern its twelve keys share, which is also the pattern Slack's own launch guidance follows:

  1. Client-specific over generic. The option that starts from the client's goals, structure or pain points beats the one that applies a template.
  2. Measurable over aspirational. A vision statement with success metrics beats one without.
  3. Hands-on over told. Experiential learning is the outline's own phrase: users doing real work in Slack during the session beats a demo or a manual.
  4. Phased and ongoing over one-off. Basic then advanced, with refreshers and post-launch support, beats a single big training day.
  5. Balanced over extreme. "Core global settings, regional adjustments on request" (c2a30421) beats both "identical everywhere" and "each region on its own".
The five reworded pairs are redundancy, not error

1d57f94c/ff8a88a6 (Org Owner), 60708c05/cb206cbe (Workspace Admin), ad687de6/016036e7 (naming conventions), 7cdb2278/0609296f (hands-on workshop) and 535baa2b/6b1ec162 (channel analytics) ask the same thing with the same keyed answer. Both copies are correct. The duplicate detector cannot see them because the stems were reworded, not copied.

04Delivery and Migration

17% of the exam — about ten questions — and three deck items. The deck has a first step (a needs analysis, 96be8a94), a rule for who the client calls (663ae7a0) and an after-care step (6b9124ed). The migration itself is missing.

Roles on an engagement

The outline's second bullet is the division of labour between Slack, the Partner and the client. The deck's one item keys the answer the outline implies: in a partner-led migration, the Partner's team is the client's first contact and escalates to Slack as needed (663ae7a0). The client owns decisions and internal comms; the Partner runs the project; Slack supplies the platform and its own support.

What the deck skips: the migration itself

No Slack page lays out a consultant's migration method, so this is practice, not documentation — but it is exactly what the outline's bullets name:

PhaseThe outline's wordsWhat it means in practice
Pre-migration"scheduling, user cleanup, comms"Agree the cutover window and any content freeze; clean the user list (leavers deactivated, duplicates merged, emails matching the identity provider); tell users what changes and when
Execution"migration status, migration issues, coordination with the client and Slack"Track progress against the plan, triage and log issues, keep one channel of communication with the client and one with Slack
After(the deck's 6b9124ed)Support, extra training, check-ins — not "hand over and leave"

The documented part is Slack's import and export tools: a standard export covers public channels; a self-serve export of private channels and DMs needs Business+ or Enterprise; and on Enterprise the Discovery API is the route eDiscovery partners use.

05Discovery

10% of the exam — about six questions — and four deck items, three of them judgment. The deck's count is right; what it cannot give you is practice against plausible options.

  • Articulate the value of Slack. The deck's one decidable item keys integrations with the tools the client already uses (4953a8af) — Slack's own guide to apps frames it the same way.
  • Ask about long-term goals and current pain, not features (70cfc31f, a401a105).
  • A vision statement needs specific, measurable goals tied to business objectives (4c998d54). The outline pairs it with success metrics, which no deck item names: adoption (weekly active members), engagement (messages in channels rather than DMs), and a business metric the client already tracks.
Two discovery bullets the deck never asks

Research the client before the first meeting — their structure, their current tools and ways of working — so the questions are informed. And ask about security and policy: existing controls, compliance obligations, the desired future state. That second one feeds straight into §7's settings, and an exam item that pairs a regulated client with a discovery question will want it.

06Grid Design

15% of the exam — about nine questions — and two deck items. Proportionally the deck's thinnest domain after Governance.

"Grid" is Enterprise Grid: one organisation holding several workspaces. Org-level policies apply across every workspace, and workspace-level settings sit underneath them. A grid design decides how many workspaces there are, what each one is for, and which settings are fixed at the org and which are left to workspaces.

  • Mirror the organisation's structure — departments, projects, regions — rather than a generic template (a0bf9e87).
  • Fix the core at org level, allow requested variation below it (c2a30421).
What the outline asks and the deck does not

The four grid bullets are a sequence: use discovery outputs to make an initial recommendation, know the best practices, lay out feasible options with their trade-offs, and support final sign-off. The deck tests the second only. Expect items that give you discovery findings and ask which design they imply, or ask what a consultant does when stakeholders cannot agree — present options with trade-offs, do not decide for them.

07Policies and Settings

22% of the exam — about 13 questions — and 13 deck items, none of them judgment. This is the deck's strongest domain and where its fact-check did most of its work. Three items were keyed on things Slack does not do, and each is worth learning as a fact in its own right.

1 · Compliance Exports do not preserve anything — 296ced65

The item asks which feature suits a firm under legal hold and keys "Compliance Exports". That name is not current Slack terminology, and an export retrieves data; it does not preserve it. Slack's actual mechanism is the native Legal Holds feature: on Enterprise, a member with the Legal Holds Admin role places a hold on specific people, and their messages and files are kept regardless of retention settings and regardless of edits or deletions. Held data is then read through an export or the Discovery API. Legal Holds is not among the item's options, so the key stands as the best of four — but on a real engagement, and on an exam item that offers it, the answer is Legal Holds.

2 · There is no IP allowlist for member sign-in — 10bdf793

The item keys "IP whitelisting to restrict access to the office network". Slack's Help Center documents no self-serve setting that limits where members sign in from. The network control it does document is proxy-based: your proxy inserts X-Slack-Allowed-Workspaces-Requester and X-Slack-Allowed-Workspaces headers, which limit which workspaces people on your network can reach — the reverse direction. IP allowlisting exists in Slack only for app OAuth and SCIM tokens. And Slack writes "allowlist" now, not "whitelist". The key stands as the only option with a location component.

3 · There is no channel-creation approval queue — 2169924a, 47e8ec41

Slack's channel-management setting is a permission: owners choose which roles may create public channels, create private channels, archive and unarchive. There is no propose-and-approve queue for new channels, no "justification" prompt on creation, and no automatic archiving of inactive channels — archiving is manual, one at a time or in bulk. Slack does ship approval queues, but for Slack Connect invitations, workspace invitations and app requests. 2169924a keeps its "propose, subject to approval" key as an organisational process (run through Workflow Builder or a request channel); 47e8ec41 had its key moved C → A because its keyed "justification" setting does not exist.

Administrative roles

RoleWhat sets it apart
Org Primary OwnerExactly one per Enterprise org; the only one who can transfer ownership
Org OwnerEverything the Primary Owner can do except transfer ownership — the top role you assign (1d57f94c, ff8a88a6)
Org AdminManages org settings and policies, below the owners
Workspace OwnerTop of a single workspace; appoints Workspace Admins
Workspace AdminManages members and settings, but "can't access the Billing page" (60708c05, cb206cbe)

Slack documents no "Guest Admin" and no "User Group Admin". Guests are multi-channel or single-channel account types; managing user groups is a permission.

Identity and org-level security

  • SCIM provisioning ties accounts to the directory, so a leaver is deactivated automatically (d1ec6012).
  • SAML SSO — not in the deck. Members sign in through your identity provider. Set up by Workspace or Org Owners; Business+ and Enterprise plans (or Free and Pro with a connected Salesforce org).
  • Session duration — not in the deck. On all plans, owners and admins can sign members out on close or after a set time. Members are warned two hours before and again 15 minutes before; a change ends existing sessions at random across the chosen period rather than all at once.

Compliance, DLP, Slack Connect, apps

  • DLP is Enterprise only. It scans messages, text files and canvases, and a DLP Admin can aim a rule at Slack Connect conversations (d1458b4b).
  • Slack Connect approval settings — not in the deck. On paid plans, owners and admins set who approves Slack Connect requests, when approval is required, where requests go, and when invitations are accepted automatically.
  • App approval. By default members can install apps without approval. Owners turn approval on, pre-approve or restrict apps, and appoint App Managers. For a regulated client, require a security review per app and route installs through IT (6bfcfbed, 79e98f8f). Slack reviews Marketplace apps but says it "doesn't endorse or certify" them — a listing is not assurance.
  • Analytics. The Channels tab shows creation date, membership, messages posted, members who posted and viewed, reactions and huddles — on Pro and Business+, public channels only (535baa2b, 6b1ec162).

08Channel Strategy

10% of the exam — about six questions — and three deck items, all about naming and project channels. The domain's fourth bullet, workflows, has none.

  • Naming conventions with consistent prefixes by department, function or project make channels findable (ad687de6, 016036e7).
  • A channel per project centralises its conversation and files (6febbd25).
Workflows — named in the outline, absent from the deck

The outline says to educate the client on Slack workflows and names two: an onboarding workflow and a standup workflow. They are built in Workflow Builder, which automates everyday processes in Slack and can connect to other apps. By default all members can create workflows and members and guests can use them; it is on paid plans; and you start from a template or from scratch. An onboarding workflow greets a new member in a channel and hands them what to read; a standup workflow posts the same questions on a schedule and collects the answers. Expect an item asking which Slack capability answers "we want every new joiner to get the same welcome" — the answer is a workflow, not a pinned message or a bot you build.

09Governance Structure (Owner and Admin Roles)

8% of the exam — about five questions — and two deck items, which are one question asked twice (who gets the top role: the Org Owner). The domain's other two bullets have nothing.

The admin support model — not in the deck

The outline asks you to "facilitate admin support model discussions (for example: request/help/approvals process flows)". No Slack page prescribes one, so this is practice: decide where members ask for help (a help channel), how they request things (a request workflow rather than a DM to whoever is an admin), and who approves what. The concrete approvals are the ones Slack actually queues — app requests, workspace invitations and Slack Connect invitations — plus whatever you have restricted by permission, such as channel creation.

Admin processes that can move into Slack — not in the deck

The third bullet asks which of a client's admin processes and workflows can be migrated into Slack. The answer shape is the same as §8: request-and-approve processes that currently run on email or forms become Workflow Builder workflows with a form step and a channel for the approver.

Assigning a role is Governance; knowing its permissions is Policies

This guide files "who should be assigned the top role" under Governance and "which role can do X without billing access" under Policies and Settings, following the outline's own wording. On the exam it rarely matters which domain a role question belongs to, but it does matter that you know both the table in §7 and why a governance team is chosen before settings are.

10Learning and Enablement

18% of the exam — about eleven questions — and ten deck items, eight of them judgment. Read §3's five-point pattern before these; it is the only thing that transfers.

  • The experiential learning approach: users learn by doing real or simulated work in Slack, with guidance — a hands-on workshop, a mock project, a role-play of the team's own process (20885bb7, 0609296f, 24d2ac86, 7cdb2278).
  • Curricula differ for admins and users: admins get settings, security and governance on top of the basics (0c94d85c); users get a path from basics to advanced, tailored to their role (eaa94ffb).
  • Phased and ongoing: initial, then advanced, then refreshers as Slack changes (9f6ca95b, bd98bac0).

The two decidable items: user troubleshooting

SymptomFirst step
Cannot hear others on a callCheck Slack's audio settings and that the speakers or headphones are connected and selected (e50e9a46)
Slow loading, frequent disconnectsClear the app's cache (d7360bcf), per Slack's connection troubleshooting page
Two bullets the deck skips

Create learning materials — quick-reference guides, short videos, a channel of tips — to support the curriculum after the live sessions. And recommend a rollout communication plan, then enable the client to run it: what users are told before launch, on launch day and after, and who sends it. Note the verb — the consultant enables the client's comms; they do not become the client's comms team.

12Distractor tells

Read the question's own verb

"Which setting" wants something that exists in Slack's admin pages — test every option against that. "Which approach" or "what should the consultant do" is a judgment item: apply §3's pattern. "Which role" wants a role that exists.

  • A setting that sounds balanced but does not exist. Justification on channel creation, automatic archiving of inactive channels, an approval queue for channels, an IP allowlist for sign-in. The deck's three corrections were all this shape.
  • A role that does not exist. Guest Admin, User Group Admin.
  • An export offered as preservation. Exports retrieve; Legal Holds preserve.
  • Two-factor authentication as the answer to a location or compliance problem. It proves who, not where, and preserves nothing.
  • The extreme option in a judgment item — admins only, or no rules at all; identical everywhere, or everyone for themselves.
  • A plan-tier trap. DLP and Legal Holds on a Pro workspace are not available.

How to verify any of this yourself

slack.com/help fails honestly: an invented article id returns a real HTTP 404, so a status check works. But the URL slug is cosmetic — the article resolves by its number alone, and a bare slack.com/help/articles/<id> redirects to the current slug. So a stale slug is not a dead link, and a plausible one is no evidence. Check the rendered title.

14Two-week revision plan

The deck takes under an hour, so it cannot anchor two weeks. Week 1 learns the facts and the missing bullets; week 2 practises judgment, which the deck can only partly help with.

Week 1 — the facts, and what the deck skips

  1. Day 1 · Diagnostic. Read the exam guide's outline. Do all 37 deck questions; note the misses by the three kinds in §3.
  2. Day 2–3 · Policies and Settings. §7 in full: the three corrections, the roles table, SSO, session duration, Slack Connect approvals, app approval, DLP. Then the deck's 13 items. This is the domain where knowledge alone scores.
  3. Day 4 · Channels and workflows. §8. Build an onboarding workflow and a standup workflow in a free trial workspace if you can.
  4. Day 5 · Grid and governance. §6 and §9. Sketch a grid for a three-region company and its admin support model.
  5. Day 6 · Migration. §4. Write a one-page pre-migration checklist from memory.
  6. Day 7 · Consolidate. §11 and §12.

Week 2 — judgment

  1. Day 8 · Discovery. §5. Write a vision statement with three success metrics for an imaginary client.
  2. Day 9–10 · Learning and Enablement. §10, then draft an admin curriculum and a rollout comms plan.
  3. Day 11 · Generate harder judgment items (§16) with four plausible options each, and answer them with §3's pattern.
  4. Day 12 · §13 in full.
  5. Day 13 · Full deck in Blitz mode, options read aloud off.
  6. Day 14 · Only the misses, and the uncovered table in §2 as a checklist.
